# Model Scope

Are We Alone in the Universe? Earth-like Planet Calculator is a browser-based probabilistic modelling calculator.

It explores how astrophysical, planetary, geophysical, biochemical, and optional speculative assumptions affect estimates of modelled Earth-like candidates across the Milky Way and the observable universe.

Outputs are conditional on the selected assumptions, parameter ranges, presets, and uncertainty settings. Some factors are better constrained by astronomical literature, while others are weakly constrained or explicitly speculative.

The observable-universe scaling panel uses an order-of-magnitude star-count range, currently `10^22` to `10^24` stars, rather than multiplying by a fixed number of galaxies. Deterministic mode scales the current scenario point by its per-star yield; Monte Carlo mode scales the per-sample yield `N_i / N_GHZ_i` so sampled GHZ-star counts do not distort the extrapolation. This is transparent scenario scaling, not a direct census, and it does not model galaxy type, cosmic epoch, metallicity evolution, or low-mass-star uncertainties.

It should not be interpreted as:

- a deterministic prediction,
- an empirical census of planets,
- an observational exoplanet catalogue,
- proof of extraterrestrial life,
- proof of absence of extraterrestrial life,
- an estimate of confirmed civilizations,
- an estimate of confirmed communicative civilizations,
- a substitute for peer-reviewed exoplanet surveys or mission-based astronomical datasets,
- a settled scientific consensus.

The calculator is intended to support transparency, inspection, comparison, and structured reasoning under uncertainty. It makes assumptions, uncertainty, and scale visible, adjustable, and inspectable without claiming empirical confirmation of life, civilizations, or detectability.
